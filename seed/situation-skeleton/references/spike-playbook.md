# Spike playbook

A timeboxed empirical answer to a question that can only be settled by
running something. Depth document referenced by a Breadcrumb vertex.

A spike is not research (a question a read answers) and not a deliverable:
it defends no promise and earns no completion gate. It exists to collapse
or confirm one unknown, cheaply.

## Discipline

1. State the hypothesis before anything runs. A question with no hypothesis
   is not a spike.

2. Freeze the criteria before the results exist. RESULT is judged strictly
   against pre-declared CRITERIA, never against a moved target. Write the
   criteria, then run.

3. Classify honestly against the frozen criteria:
   - DEMONSTRATED — existence proof only, nothing more.
   - REFUTED — decisive miss: the hypothesis did not survive.
   - INCONCLUSIVE — it did not decide; say why.
   An expired timebox is INCONCLUSIVE with what you have, not a license to
   keep running.

4. A refuted hypothesis is a successful spike. It collapses a possibility;
   refutation is an earned answer, not a failure.

5. Every spike runs under a timebox; the overall inquiry runs under a
   decision point. When the point arrives, the inquiry resolves — promote,
   discard, or park — even if a tail remains.

6. Keep the rig quarantined from product paths. A spike rig is disposable
   test apparatus: it never ships and never touches reviewed source.

7. Keep the raw evidence that exercised the criteria — exact commands, rig,
   run count, decisive output. A bare verdict without that evidence is
   inadmissible.

8. Mixed outcomes stay mixed. Never round a partially supporting run toward
   a verdict.

9. Wrong-early is expected and visible. Pre-decisive attempts are corrected
   and retained in the record, not sanitized. An unchanged question,
   criteria, and method between runs is noise and gets no second run: every
   element of the plan must differ to justify a follow-up.

The discipline is small: hypothesis, frozen criteria, timebox, one decisive
observable, honest classification, raw evidence. Anything around it —
charters, ledgers, role passes — is ceremony, not the method.
